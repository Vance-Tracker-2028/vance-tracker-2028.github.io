Myth 1 · The whole story

That Hillbilly Elegy describes a sexual encounter with a couch

False: the passage isn’t in the book.

Last updated November 27, 2026, 8:16 AM PT.

What they said

On July 15, 2024 — the day Donald Trump named Vance as his running mate — an X user, @rickrudescalves, claimed that Vance’s 2016 memoir admitted, on pages 179–181, to a sexual act involving “an inside-out latex glove shoved between two couch cushions.”

Who spread it: the original X post, then other users who repeated it as if it were in the book. Illinois Gov. JB Pritzker amplified it on television, telling ABC’s This Week on July 28, 2024, that Trump’s running mate was “getting known for his obsession with couches.” He also joked about a “Couches Against Trump” Zoom at a July 29 fundraiser. A later variant circulated a fake page said to be from a first edition or galley proof.

What is true

The passage does not exist, in the 2016 edition or in the first edition. Snopes rated the original rumor false. PolitiFact, reviewing Pritzker’s repetition, rated it Pants on Fire and said the claim “surfaced on social media in a satirical post.”

PolitiFact searched a digital copy of the 2016 book and found ten uses of the word “couch,” none in the sense of the rumor, and no uses of “sofa,” “lounge,” “settee,” or “love seat.” It then read pages 179–181 in a 2016 paperback. Those pages describe Vance’s arrival at Ohio State, orientation, Columbus’s proximity to Middletown, roommates, financial-aid forms, an interest in law school, and a job at the Ohio Statehouse — and they do not mention a couch.

The original poster later followed the claim with the “Just Go On the Internet and Tell Lies” meme, which Snopes, citing Know Your Meme, treated as a signal that he was joking. NewsGuard, cited by PolitiFact, also traced the claim to a satirical post.

A later image, passed off as a first-edition or galley page about the “eroticism” of couch cushions, was fake. Snopes reported that an X user who circulated scans later described them as “a simple edit of an e-book copy.” The book still contains no such passage.

The Associated Press published a fact-check of the rumor on July 24, 2024, then removed it. The notice on the page, as reported by Snopes, said the story “did not go through our standard editing process and has been removed.” That withdrawal was about AP’s editing process, not a finding that the rumor was true.
