Methodology

A critic is listed only if we opened a post, interview, editorial, or official statement, and if the account has at least 50,000 X followers or is tied to a major media outlet, think tank, campaign, PAC, party body, elected office, or advocacy organization.

The list is assembled from three sources: (a) authors of critical items already in the tracker; (b) prominent accounts in the replies and quote-posts under those items, including the Matt Walsh poll thread of November 17–18, 2026; and (c) a fresh X and web search for high-reach critics on the right and the left. Ranking score equals rank reach times (1 + 0.25 × (critical items − 1)). For CUFI Action Fund, rank reach is the parent @CUFI follower count, which is disclosed on that card, because the Action Fund account itself is small.

“Right” and “left” describe public office, stated affiliation, or an outlet’s obvious editorial identity. “Institutional” is used for organizations that are not primarily a U.S. party office — here, the Anti-Defamation League and The Jerusalem Post. “Unspecified” is used only when the bio and opened posts do not state a partisan identity.

This is not a complete census. A missing name is not a finding that someone is not a critic. Matt Walsh is not listed; his talent poll included Vance and did not attack him. Tomi Lahren commented in that thread with a preference for Ron DeSantis, which is not treated as a critique of Vance. Sen. Tom Cotton was described in Jewish Insider as taking a “veiled swipe” without naming Vance, so he is not listed. News outlets that only reported criticism are not listed as critics; signed editorials, signed columns, and organizational statements are. Michael Doran’s Hudson Institute essay is listed; a later X post in which he said it is “simply false” that he is “against JD Vance” is noted on his card.

Independent tracker. Not affiliated with JD Vance or any campaign, political party, or government office. Polling and sentiment figures are recorded as published; authenticity ratings are heuristics, not proof.
